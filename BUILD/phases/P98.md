# P98 - A fiscal year the file states seven times

## Intent

Read the periods a real workbook states in the forms it states them, without inventing a fiscal calendar the document does not carry. A derivation from evidence in hand is not an assumption; a bare year turned into a year end is. The two must stay on opposite sides of that line, and P38's rule - a projected year must never enter the historical table - governs both.

## Tasks

- [x] Derive the fiscal year end from period ends that recur on a month end across years, even when interim stubs sit beside them, and record what it was derived from
- [x] Refuse the derivation when it would be a guess rather than a reading - too few years, or no month clearly dominant
- [x] Report yearly columns the document names but does not date, instead of reporting no period cue
- [x] Keep a projected column distinguishable from the historical year of the same name, which is the whole of P38

## Exit criteria

*The phase closes when all of these are true and `gate close` says so.*

- A workbook whose sheets are five December year ends and two February interims reports a December fiscal year end, with its evidence
- A forecast tab headed with bare years reports the years it covers and says plainly that dating them needs the engagement's year end
- Two columns headed with the same year are reported as needing to be told apart, not silently merged
- A mixed set of year ends and interim stubs still reports the fiscal year end as not stated when too few years recur, when two months are equally dominant, or when the candidate ends are not month ends
