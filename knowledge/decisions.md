# Judgment rules

`When <situation>, I <do this>, because <reason>.` One per entry, with the project
codename and session it came from. These are the raw material for skills: a rule
with no reason is a follow-up, not a rule.

- When a client's documents arrive, I fill in the historical financials first, because everything else in the model builds on them (reason inferred - confirm). (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the client provides data in Excel (historicals or forecast), I copy the relevant tabs into our model and highlight them yellow, because that shows the data came from the client. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the client provides no forecast, we build one ourselves and send it to the client to approve or reject, because (reason not given - follow-up). (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When it is a repeat client, we do largely what we did last year and adjust the forecast for what the client provides or tells us, because (reason not given - follow-up). (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When I believe the model is complete and the client has provided everything, I tell the associate, who reviews it before it goes to the MD. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the associate has reviewed the model, it goes to an internal value review with the MD, presented by me, the associate, or both, because the MD decides whether the report gets written. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the company is a normal operating business valued for the owners' information, the work is easier than a startup, PPA or buy-sell, because (reason not given - likely a revenue history and no transaction to allocate; confirm). (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When valuing almost any company, we run a DCF, a GPC analysis and guideline M&A transactions, because together they give a better idea of what is going on; one-off or custom analyses are added when the engagement calls for them. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When documents arrive over several days, I wait for the historical financials before doing much, because the model is set up on them and the client's forecast is built on top of them; other documents are used as they come. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the historicals are in, the model's checks must pass: net income in the model ties to the client's net income; the balance sheet balances; ending cash on the cash flow statement matches cash on the balance sheet. Getting them to pass takes real time. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When the company has long-term debt, every instrument is entered individually into the model's debt tab, which takes a long time. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When choosing what the system automates first, start with mature operating company valuations, because they use only the three core methods and are the easiest. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When an engagement is very complex, the MD may review the model directly as well, depending on the MD. (general, 2026-09-16-1)
- When normalizing a mature company's earnings, EBITDA adjustments get a lot of attention, but owner pay is very rarely adjusted, because (reason not given - likely market-rate pay already; confirm). (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When a company has interest income, it is usually removed from EBITDA; it comes up often. (general, 2026-09-17-1, confirmed 2026-09-17-2)
- When it is a repeat client, I use the same adjustment items as last year and fill in this year's values; when it is a new client, the adjustments have to be thought through from scratch. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When looking for EBITDA adjustments on a new client, I start in the detail of other income and expense, then the internal financials' line detail, what management says in the due diligence meeting, and the notes to audited financials if provided, because that is where one-time and discretionary items show up. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When deciding on an adjustment, it is usually the analyst's own judgment on the information available rather than something the client states. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When deciding whether an expense is one-time, I first check that it appears in only one or two periods and not in previous years, then usually ask management to confirm, because a cost that keeps coming back is part of normal operations. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When the client provides a forecast, we do not change it: it is the source of truth for our forecast, and we tie to it wherever possible, because the forecast is the client's. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When forecasting interest expense, we do not tie to the client's interest expense line; we build our own from every long-term debt instrument entered in the model's debt tab. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When the client provides no forecast, we build one in the model from drivers beneath the income statement - a revenue growth rate per year, cost of goods sold as a percentage of revenue, and operating expenses as a percentage of revenue that may improve gradually - because the model computes the lines from the drivers and a driver is easy to change. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When setting forecast drivers, we avoid big assumptions and forecast what we think the company will actually do; clients rarely push back on the result. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When setting a forecast driver, I start from the historical percentage - the last year, or an average of the last few years, whichever I believe is the normal level going forward - then step it up or down in the later forecast years. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When starting the income approach, the DCF is not built: its structure is already in the template model, and the work is filling its inputs. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When working the pilot, guideline public companies, guideline M&A transactions and WACC data are independent of each other and can be done side by side. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When the DCF uses the terminal (exit) multiple method rather than Gordon Growth, the medians and averages of the GPC and transaction multiples - EV/EBITDA and EV/revenue - support the multiple chosen. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When building the comps and transactions sets, the data is pulled by hand from Capital IQ, because the firm does not pay for its API. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When deciding to drop a comparable company or transaction, it goes only if its financial situation has changed a lot or it has left the subject company's line of business. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When the three methods are done, they are not weighted: each gives an enterprise value range, and a final enterprise value is chosen somewhere within the ranges - ultimately by the managing director. (general, 2026-09-17-1)
- When a model check fails, it is almost always our own mistake in the model, and the cause varies; finding it needs the model itself. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When filling the debt tab, I take each long-term debt instrument from the debt note in the audited financials and enter its maturity date, starting amount, years remaining and interest rate, because that note describes every instrument in one place. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When there is no audited debt note, we either match the client's interest expense, ask the client for the long-term debt details, or - if nothing is provided - forecast interest expense at zero or flat. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When the DCF uses an exit multiple, the MD picks the multiple, usually around the median and average of the comps' multiples. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When there is a good comp set the team is confident in, the DCF's terminal value uses the exit multiple method; many non-ESOP DCFs use it. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When there is a good comp set, cost of equity uses CAPM; without one, the build-up method. A repeat client does what was done last year; who decides otherwise is not clear. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When building WACC, market data comes from Kroll, but beta is the median unlevered beta of the comps from Capital IQ, relevered to the subject company - so WACC is not final until the comp set is in. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When setting the pretax cost of debt, we decide what credit rating the subject company would have, then use the cost of debt for that rating. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When pulling data, the comps, transactions and WACC inputs can be gathered side by side, but the WACC waits for the final comp set. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When choosing the credit rating for cost of debt, today we eyeball it and the MD decides; the cost of debt for each rating comes from Capital IQ. The analyst would like the call supported by comparing interest coverage, debt to equity and similar measures. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When it is a repeat client, we copy last year's historical financials into the new model and enter only the new period; forecast with the same methodology but fresh assumptions; pull new WACC data as of the new valuation date; keep the comps unless there is good reason to change them; and carry last year's adjustment items with new amounts. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When audited and internal financials disagree for the same year, we use the audited ones, because they are more trustworthy; the forecast is always the client's internal one, because there is never an audited forecast. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When a document arrives late or revised, we use the most recent one received; if it restates past periods, we update those numbers in the model too. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When an older audited statement and a newer internal one disagree about the same year, the audited one probably wins - the analyst is not certain; confirm with an associate or the MD. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When the model reaches the internal value review, the MD looks first at the multiples used in the M&A transactions method, then the comps, then what is happening in the subject company's industry and market. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When the internal value review is done, usually little changes; if anything, the multiples used or a WACC assumption such as the company-specific risk premium. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When valuing a mature operating company, the standard of value is usually fair market value, on a controlling basis. (general, 2026-09-17-2)
- When relevering the comps' median unlevered beta, the target capital structure for the subject company is used, and the model does it automatically - so the formula lives in the Excel template, not in the analyst's hands. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When deciding what this system is for in version 1: gathering data and putting it into the same format every time, so that inputting it into the model is easy. **Version 1 does not run the model.** Data collection and organization is the goal. (scope, 2026-09-18-1)
- When version 1 finishes, it fills an "input help" workbook that matches the format of the model's inputs and does not touch the real model, because the real model is only integrated once the workbook works extremely well. (scope, 2026-09-18-1)
- When version 1 fills the input-help workbook, the tabs that matter are Input, WS, Sales Detail, IS, BS and D; CF is skipped because it is sometimes hardcoded and sometimes formula-built, and the rest of the model is formula driven. (scope, 2026-09-18-1)
- When it is a repeat client, the Input tab is easy: last year's model already holds the answers, so the system finds last year's model and repeats the inputs. When it is a new valuation, the same fields have to be found in the client documents. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When it is a repeat client, the WS and Sales Detail tabs are copied across and the years shifted over, with the new year found in the client documents and added in the same format; when it is a new client, WS is where the income statement breakout is built from scratch. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When the WS tab holds a supporting breakout, the IS tab's line item is linked to the total line on WS, so the breakout is the source and the IS line is the sum. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When a cell in the model is yellow, a person types into it - the same yellow convention used for data that came from the client. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When counting the work, about 75% of engagements are repeat clients, so the repeat path - last year's model copied forward with the years shifted - is the one that carries most of the value. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When setting up a repeat engagement, I point the system at last year's model's Box file location myself, rather than expecting it to find the file. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When a client's line items change between years - which is not often - I work out what to do case by case, and this is something the system should stop and ask about rather than resolve. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When a line item does not match last year, the first question is whether the client actually changed it or whether we simply grouped it differently last year, so the comparison is against last year's model grouping (the WS tab) and not only against the client's documents. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When starting a repeat client's WS tab, I copy and paste the previous year's WS tab into the new workbook, shift the years over, and add the new values for the line items that are already set - the line items themselves are carried forward, not re-decided. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When filling the WS tab, the values come from the same audited or internal financials as the income statement; WS is the breakout of detail behind an IS line rather than a separate source, used when a client has more detail lines than the IS has slots. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When setting up a repeat engagement's Input tab, the only fields I change are the valuation date and the latest fiscal year end, usually to one year later; everything else carries forward from last year's model. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When rolling a repeat client forward, the historical block is a fixed ten columns and the oldest year falls off; it does not grow. In practice only the most recent five years are filled, though there are spots for ten. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When an engagement starts, we send the client a due diligence request list in seven lettered sections, and that same workbook is the tracker - each item carries In Model?, notes, Provided and Not Applicable, and supporting data gets a tab named after the item number. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When asking for financial records, the request is for five fiscal years, which is why only five historical years are filled in the model. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When looking for EBITDA adjustments, the request list asks the client outright for compensation detail by owner, discretionary payments and one-time or non-operational items - so the add-back candidates are partly supplied, not only inferred from statement detail. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When building a due diligence request list, the section groupings stay the same every time (A financials, B corporate documents, C operational, and so on) while the items within each section change per engagement; for a repeat client the list usually stays similar year to year. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When documents are collected, each request-list section is a folder in Box and a folder in ShareFile, so a document's section is known from where it sits rather than from classifying it. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When client documents arrive in ShareFile, I download them by hand and move them into the Box folders; a Power Automate flow to do this automatically is being built, and the tool should pick up from the Box folder rather than reaching into ShareFile itself. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When the client provides PDFs, the numbers are entered by hand and that is the hardest and slowest path; when they provide an Excel workbook, the tab is moved into the model and linked up, which is easy. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When the client sends PDFs, they are scans without a text layer, so nothing can be selected or copied and every figure is keyed by hand. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When clients send documents, a lot of them are PDFs and none of those can be copied from, so OCR sits in front of most of the inbound path rather than at its edge. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When keying the historicals out of scanned PDFs into the model, it takes one hour and sometimes three, depending on how many documents there are. That is the time version 1 is meant to save. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When checking extracted data, I would otherwise double-check every figure against the client documents, and the data needs to be 100% accurate every time; but a figure that carries the page and row it came from, whose totals all re-add and whose statements tie, is enough - I would not go back to the PDF. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
- When a figure is not covered by any arithmetic check, I want to see every one of them, not only the ones that changed since last year. (general, 2026-09-18-1)
