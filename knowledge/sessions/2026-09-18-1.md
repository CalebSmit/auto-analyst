## Q6 - How often do the client's line items change between years?

**Answer.** Not often. When it does: "I just figure out what to do. **That is something you
would stop and ask about.** Or in the model, figure out if it actually changed - or if we just
**grouped something differently**."

**Two things in that answer, and the second is the sharper one.**

1. A changed line item is a **review item**, not something the system resolves. That is the
   confidence router and review queue the build already has, used for exactly what it was
   built for.
2. A mismatch has **two possible causes and they are not the same**: the client changed their
   accounts, or *the firm* grouped them differently last year. So the comparison is not
   new-documents-against-old-documents; it is **new documents against last year's model
   grouping**, which is the WS tab. The firm's own grouping is half the answer and it lives in
   the prior-year workbook.

**What this changes in the build - version 1 may need very little of a model.** If line items
are stable across years for three quarters of the work, the repeat path is mostly
*deterministic*: read last year's WS grouping, match this year's accounts to it, fill the new
column, and raise a review item on anything that does not match. That is copying and matching,
not judgement. It fits the non-negotiables exactly - a skill proposes and never acts, and only
a human decision settles the ambiguous case - and it means version 1 could run with the model
doing far less than the current recipes assume.

## Q7 - What tells you where each account belongs on WS for the new year?

**Answer.** "I usually copy and paste the previous year WS tab into the new one. Then just
shift the years over, and add the new values for the already set line items."

**So the line items are not re-decided. They are carried forward, and the year is filled in.**

The version 1 operation for WS, in full:

1. Read last year's WS tab: the line item labels, and each one's value per historical year.
2. Shift the year columns.
3. For each **already-set** line item, find this year's value in the client documents.
4. Anything that cannot be found, or anything in the documents that maps to no line item,

**A finding against the build, and it is a significant one.** The build tags line items against

*this client's own WS labels from last year*, which are the firm's grouping decisions for that
engagement, not a firm-wide taxonomy. For 75% of the work the prior workbook *is* the chart of
accounts. The firm-wide taxonomy is the fallback for the new-client quarter.

## Q10 - Does the year window roll or grow?

## Q15 - Are those PDFs text or scans?

**Answer.** **Scans.** The text cannot be selected.

### Finding 1: the highest-value path produces nothing today

`intake/readers/documents.py` says it outright: *"Scanned pages (no text layer) are reported,
not OCR'd: the OCR path is a later adapter, and a page that yields nothing is a fact the
manifest should carry rather than a blank the pipeline fills in."* That is the build behaving
well - it refuses to invent - but combined with Q14 it means **the only document path with real
keying in it is the one the reader cannot read**. Version 1's value depends on an adapter that
does not exist.

### Finding 2: OCR has an ordering problem, and it is a decision, not a task

The guarantee is that a model only ever sees masked payloads (`data_sensitivity: masked_only`,
and the Policy Gate enforces it). Masking needs text; text from a scan needs OCR. So:

- **OCR by a model** - sending the page image to a vision model - puts **unmasked client
  documents in front of a model before masking can happen**. That breaks the guarantee at its
  root, and no guard downstream can recover it.
- **OCR locally and deterministically** - Tesseract or similar, on this machine - keeps the
  ordering intact: image -> text (local) -> mask -> model. It also keeps `privacy/`
  deterministic Python throughout, as the blueprint requires.

So the OCR adapter should be local. That is a real dependency decision (a new binary, its
licence, its accuracy on the firm's scans) and it belongs to a person - recorded for
BUILD/OPEN.md rather than chosen here.

### Finding 3: accuracy stops being a detail

Session 3 queued a 95% extraction-accuracy bar. On clean Excel that is easy; on OCR'd scans it
is the whole problem, and it is why the analyst's hand-keyed engagement as a gold case
(follow-up 9) matters more than anything else queued.

## Q16 - Which documents arrive as scans?

**Answer.** **Any PDF they get cannot be copied from** - so every PDF is effectively a scan -
**"and the clients send a lot of PDFs."**

So OCR is not an edge case for awkward documents; it is the main inbound path. Q14's "PDFs are

## Q18-19 - What would you check, and what would be enough?

**Q18 answer.** "I don't want to double check it with the client docs, but that is what I would

**Q19 answer.** Asked whether a figure carrying its page and row, whose totals all re-add and

### This settles the auto-accept bar, which has been open since the start

`BUILD/OPEN.md` §2 lists "Set materiality thresholds and the auto-accept confidence bar" as a
decision belonging to a person, and records that **no answer exists**, so today every item goes
to review - safe and noisy. For extracted figures the answer is now given, and it is **not a
confidence number**:

> **A figure may be accepted without a person looking at it when it carries its source locator
> and every total it feeds re-adds correctly and the statements tie. Otherwise a person sees
> it.**

That is a rule about *evidence*, not about a model's self-reported probability - which is the
right shape, because a confident OCR misread is exactly the failure a probability cannot catch
and a failed footing can.

### What it means for the build

- The **tie-out guard** and `tools/statement_checks.py` stop being quality reporting and become
  **the acceptance mechanism**. Their coverage decides how much the analyst has to look at.
- The **provenance gate** is what makes the residual check cheap: two seconds, not two minutes.
- **The residual set is figures no arithmetic covers.** Most income statement and balance sheet
  lines feed a total that re-adds, so they are covered. Dates, names, tax status, and every
  field on the debt tab - rate, maturity, term - are not. Those are the review queue, and the
  design should aim at shrinking that set rather than at raising a confidence score.

## Q20 - For figures no arithmetic covers, see all or only the changed ones?

**Answer.** **Every one of them** - not only the ones that changed from last year.

So the review set is defined exactly: **everything a tie-out does not cover is shown, always.**
No "unchanged since last year" shortcut, which matters most on the repeat path where almost
nothing changes and the temptation to skip is strongest. It is a small set - Input tab dates
and flags, tax status, and the debt tab's per-instrument fields - and it is bounded, so showing
all of it is cheap and the guarantee stays simple: *every figure is either proved by arithmetic
or seen by you.*
