## Q5 (queue 3)

**Asked:** When the client sends no forecast, what do you build yours from, and how does the
client approve or push back on it?

**Answer (as given):**
> It really depends because we forecast it off of the drivers beneath the income statement in
> our model, like percentage of growth per year for revenue and then for cost of goods sold,
> like the percent. of revenue moving forward that it is, and then, you know, for operating
> expenses, maybe we just say, oh, they're going to gradually get better over time, so we,
> you know, change the percentage in the forecast of revenue based on that. But then when we
> send it to a client, they don't usually uh, argue too much. It's pretty, it's pretty easy to
> change. No, we don't make too big of assumptions, just forecast it based off of what we
> think the company is going to do in the future.

**Files provided:** none.

**How a firm-built forecast is made:** in the model, from drivers beneath the income
statement:
- revenue: a growth percentage per year;
- cost of goods sold: a percentage of revenue going forward;
- operating expenses: a percentage of revenue, sometimes stepped to improve gradually over
  the forecast.
Assumptions are kept modest - what the company is expected to do, not bold calls. The client
rarely pushes back, and changing a driver is easy.

**For the build:** the forecast is driver cells in the template, so a forecast skill would
propose drivers (a handful of percentages per year, each with its reason), never the forecast
lines themselves - the model computes those, and a person reviews every assumption
(`assumptions_require_review`). The drivers are named inputs the template registration must
expose.

**Recorded:** decisions.md (+2); workflow.md (forecast step); backlog.md (B-006 note).
Coverage: forecast -> partial.

## Q6 (thread from Q5)

**Asked:** When you pick those percentages, what do you base them on - the average of the
past few years, the most recent year, industry data, or something management said?

**Answer (as given):**
> Ya usually base them on an average from the last year, or few years, depending what we
> beleive a normal % is going forward, then step up or down that % in the future years
> moving forward

**Files provided:** none.

**The rule:** a driver's starting level is the historical percentage - the last year alone, or
an average of the last few years, whichever the analyst believes is the normal level going
forward - then stepped up or down across the later forecast years.

**For the build:** the mechanical half is entirely computable. From the normalized historicals
a deterministic tool can produce, per driver, the last-year value and the multi-year averages,
with the rows each came from. The judgment is two choices: which baseline is "normal", and the
step path. That is what a forecast skill proposes, with a reason, for a person to review.

**Recorded:** decisions.md (+1); backlog.md (B-006 note). Coverage: forecast -> partial.

## Q8 (queue 5)

**Asked:** Is the DCF set up before you have WACC and filled in later? Do the GPC comps feed
the WACC? Could comps, transactions and WACC be done side by side?

**Answer (as given):**
> The dcf is already set up in the template model before anything. And yes those could all be
> done side by side. Medians and averages the GPC multiples and M&A transaction multiples, like
> the EBITDA multiples and revenue multiples, would be used to help support the multiple we
> choose in the dcf if we choose to use the terminal multiple method rather than the Gordon
> Growth method.

**Files provided:** none.

**Settled:**

  filling its inputs.

**Against the build - a recipe correction:** `recipes/mature-company-valuation.yaml` has

**Answer (as given):**
> they are not weighted. We decide ranges for each one for the enterprise value, then we look

> somewhere. But that is ultimately up to the managing director.

Then: asked for one more question to end the session.

**Files provided:** none.

**Settled:** no weighting. Each method yields an enterprise value range; the team looks across
the ranges and a final enterprise value is chosen somewhere within them; the managing director
decides.

**Matches the build, exactly:** nothing here concludes a value. The DCF skill's contract
requires a two-element range, never a point value, and no schema has a field a concluded value
could be recorded in. The system's job ends at presenting the three ranges with their support;
the choice within them is the MD's.
