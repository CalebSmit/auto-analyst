## Q1

**Asked:** In a sentence or two, what's your role in the non-ESOP valuation group? Your
title, roughly how long you've been doing it, and which parts of an engagement are yours
from start to finish.

**Answer (as given):**
> I am an analyst, and i have been doing it for about 3 months now. So i have a pretty good
> idea of how everything works. And the parts of the engagement that are mine are this: get
> all of the data that the client uploads to sharefile, and then download it into our
> internal box files. Then from there I start setting up the model. I am responsible for
> completing the entire model, all the forecasts, wacc data, gpc comps and model, m&a
> transactions and model, and then I also just did a tangible asset model as well. And alot
> of them are repeat clients, where we just do similar things that we did last year, but
> forecast it slightly different based off what the client gives us for a forecast or what
> they tell us. But then sometimes we have to make it ourselves, then send it to them, and
> they approve or not. And filling in the historical financials in the model is first
> priority, because they give us a lot of different documents, like seperate pdfs for all
> years of financials, then sometimes the interim financials. But sometimes they give us the
> historicals in excel. And they usually give the forecast in excel if they give us one. We
> copy the appropriate tabs from what they give us into our own model, and just highlight
> them yellow to show that they are from the client. then I have to present to the MD when I
> think the model is done and the client has provided all the information.

**Files provided:** none.

**Recorded:** profile.md (role, tenure, ownership, MD review); workflow.md (steps 1-7 first

## Q8

**Asked:** Of those five steps, which one or two take the most hours on a typical
mature-company valuation?

**Answer (as given):**
> The data retrieval takes the most time. Getting everything into the model. Then it is the
> forecast, and making sure all the checks in the model work, like if the net income checks
> to the client net income, and if the balance sheet balances, and if the cash at the end of
> the cash flow matches the cash on the balance sheet. Or if we have to enter in every single
> differnt lt debt instrument the company has into the models debt tab that takes a long time
> too.

**Files provided:** none.

**Where the time goes, ranked:** (1) data retrieval - getting everything into the model;
(2) the forecast; plus two named time sinks: getting the model's checks to pass, and entering
every long-term debt instrument into the model's debt tab.

**Matches the build:** timing normalization and line-item tagging target (1) directly. The
three model checks are exactly the kind of deterministic tie-out `qc-tieout` should run, and
`qc-tieout` has no mechanical half yet - a strong candidate backlog item. Debt-instrument
entry is not covered by any skill.

**Recorded:** workflow.md (hours ranking); decisions.md (+1, the three model checks);
documents.md (debt instruments); followups.md (+2). Coverage: lifecycle, qc-review,
spreading, excel-models -> partial.

**Asked:** When documents come in over several days, what can you start before everything

**Answer (as given):**
> we usually want the historical financials to come in first, because then we can set up

> financials.

**Files provided:** none.

**Rule for intake:** historical financials are the gate. Without them little can start;
with them the model is set up, and a forecast arriving later slots straight in. Everything
else is used as it comes.
