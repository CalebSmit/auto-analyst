## Q5 (resolving the Q4 contradiction)

**Asked:** Do the data pulls happen side by side, with WACC not final until the comp set is
settled when CAPM is used - or does CAPM get its betas from somewhere other than the comp set?

**Answer (as given):**
> We pull data from kroll for wacc data. But we don't pull beta from there, beta is found by
> median unlevered beta of comps pulled from cap iq. Then we relever it to our subject company.
> But ya wacc isn't final until we get all comps in. Then when we decide what the pretax cost of
> debt is. And we do that by deciding what type of credit rating it would be, then choose based
> of the cost of debt attached to that rating.

**Files provided:** none.

**Settled:**
- WACC market data comes from Kroll.
- Beta does not: it is the median unlevered beta of the comps, from Capital IQ, relevered to the
  subject company.
- So WACC is not final until the comp set is in. The pulls run side by side; the rate waits.
- Pretax cost of debt: decide the credit rating the subject company would have, then take the
  cost of debt that goes with that rating.

**Resolves the contradiction - and corrects my own change:** recipe version 2 (built at the end
of 2026-09-17-1) made `wacc` independent of `gpc`. That was wrong for any engagement using CAPM:
the beta comes from the comp set. Version 1's dependency was right for the wrong reason. To fix:
`wacc` needs `gpc` again, with the reason recorded (B-020).

**For the build:** two mechanical pieces - the median of the comps' unlevered betas and the
relevering to the subject's capital structure are arithmetic (the relevering formula is to
confirm); the credit rating is a judgment with a table lookup after it (B-021).

**Recorded:** profile.md (Kroll); decisions.md (+3); backlog.md (B-020, B-021); followups.md
(queue 9 partly answered, +2 later). Coverage: cost-of-capital, data-sources -> partial.

**Answer (as given):**

> based on the new valuation date. Comps stay the same unless there is good reason to add or get

**Files provided:** none.

**Settled - a repeat client:**

  entered from this year's documents.
- Forecast: the same methodology, not necessarily the same assumptions.

- Comps: kept, unless there is good reason to add or drop one (drop reasons: 2026-09-17-1 Q9).

**For the build:** for a repeat client the heaviest step - getting historicals in - shrinks to
one new period, if last year's model can be read as a source. The `prior-year-rollforward`
skill exists as a skeleton at `valuation/_repeat`; its declared job ("carry forward last year's

read last year's delivered workbook as the prior periods' source (B-023). The valuation date is
what the WACC data is pulled as of - an engagement profile field to confirm.

## Q8 (queue 5)

**Asked:** When audited and internal financials both arrive for the same year and disagree,
which do you use, and why?

**Answer (as given):**
> We use the audited financials, because they are more trustworthy. But will use the internal
> forecast, because there is never an audited forecast.

**Files provided:** none.

**Settled:** for historicals, audited statements win over internal ones when they disagree,
because they are more trustworthy. The forecast is always the client's internal one - there is
no such thing as an audited forecast.

**Against the build:** the timing normalizer takes no figure when two sources disagree on a line
(`tools/normalize.py`: "no figure taken") and reports the conflict for a person. That is safe,
but for this pair the analyst's rule is settled: audited over internal. The system cannot apply
it yet, because nothing records whether a financial statement is audited - the classifier has
one `financial_statements` type. Proposed (B-024): detect audited statements (an independent
auditor's report is the usual cue), prefer them in a conflict with an internal source, and keep
the disagreement visible in the alignment report rather than silently resolved.

**Recorded:** decisions.md (+1); documents.md; backlog.md (B-024). Coverage: document-types,
spreading -> partial.
